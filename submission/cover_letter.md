We submit for your consideration the manuscript "From ion channels to machine learning: an open, bilingual simulation pathway for teaching computational neuroscience to physicians and life-science students", an original article that describes and verifies a new open educational resource. Code and simulator will be available at [GitHub URL — to be added] and archived at [Zenodo DOI — to be added].

Physicians meet computational ideas every day, in sodium-channel pharmacology, electrolyte disorders, epilepsy and clinical artificial intelligence, yet few curricula let them run and question a model. NeuroSim-Edu offers a single, transparent pathway from the Hodgkin-Huxley model to a learning machine trained on the outputs of the biophysical models, and back to clinical "what-if" experiments on sodium-channel block, potassium-channel block, hyper- and hypokalaemia and loss of inhibition. Every simulator is a short NumPy loop that can be read next to its equation, every notebook and figure exists in English and Spanish, and a single command reproduces every number in the article. For classrooms without Python, the same models run in a single-file browser simulator, in English and Spanish, that works offline without installation and adds voltage-clamp and refractoriness protocols, a live classifier, guided challenges, clinical cases and a printable worksheet; its results agree with the Python package wherever the two implement the same computation.

The manuscript reports a quantitative check of each model against analytical or published benchmarks, including the first-order convergence of the Euler method. The classifier is evaluated on an independent test set of 600 newly simulated cells against a logistic-regression baseline; the two were indistinguishable in accuracy, which the article uses as a lesson in appraising clinical AI, and we state that the task is easy by construction and that the module's purpose is didactic. A comparison table (Table 3) positions the resource against published teaching tools such as Neuronify, NEST Desktop, Xolotl, Panama, MetaNeuron, Neurosim, LabAXON, Brian 2 and Neuromatch Academy: what distinguishes NeuroSim-Edu is the combination of readable code, clinical scenarios, a bridge to machine learning, bilingual material and a no-installation browser version. A dedicated section guides physicians through the material with a clinical vignette, and a bilingual practice workbook lets readers modify the code and compare their results with reference figures. We state clearly that learning outcomes have not yet been evaluated; a pilot evaluation with medical students (usability and a concept test before and after a session) is planned, and no results are claimed.

We believe the work will interest your readers because it connects membrane biophysics, clinical pharmacology and machine learning in one freely available resource, and because it lowers the language barrier for Spanish-speaking students and clinicians.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors approved the submission and declare no competing interests. The use of an AI assistant for code, text, literature search and figures is disclosed in the manuscript.
